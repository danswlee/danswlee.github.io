About

I am a postdoctoral scholar in Dan Fletcher's lab at UC Berkeley. I study the biophysical organization of the cell surface, where proteins, sugars, and other macromolecules act as a tunable biophysical barrier that shapes how cells fuse, adhere, and communicate. My training bridges statistical and soft-matter physics with quantitative cell biology: I did my Ph.D. in Quantitative and Computational Biology at Princeton, jointly advised by Cliff Brangwynne and Ned Wingreen, where I worked on the physics of biomolecular condensates and phase separation in the cell nucleus.
